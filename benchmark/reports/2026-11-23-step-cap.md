# Step caps that hold, 2026-11-23

> **Status:** Record · run 2026-11-23 (UTC+7) by the maintainer · ROADMAP focus item 1 · $0: a fake provider in the gateway's own tests · raw results: [`results/2026-11-23-step-cap`](../results/2026-11-23-step-cap/)

**In one line:** in 10 runs of 1,000 adversarial requests (10,000 in all, 500 capped steps) no step's counted spend, nor what the provider billed it, passed its cap. The first runs found a step billed 1–2 micro-dollars over its cap: the ledger rounded each part of a cost to the nearest micro-dollar while the provider and the hold rounded up. The ledger now rounds up.

## What changed

- A paid step's approved cap is a strict limit on its session (`store::step_cap`), over every key that names the session.
- Each request holds the most it could cost inside what is left: its prompt at the higher of the input and cache-write prices, and its output limit at the output price. When the request's own limit (or, without one, the model's) does not fit, the gateway lowers it to what does; less than 1,024 tokens of room is refused.
- Refused with 402 once the cap is spent; with 429 and `Retry-After: 1` while requests in flight hold the rest, since that comes back when they settle.
- A model without a price is not used in a capped step. A step whose model is not one of Pygg's gateway (`pygg/...`) is marked on the approval card: its cap is checked after the step.
- The ledger rounds each part of a cost up to the micro-dollar.

## Method

`step_cap_proof` in `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/gateway/tests.rs`: the gateway over HTTP on a store file, two client keys, and a fake provider (`obeys`) that bills a prompt of a quarter of the body's characters and as much output as the request's limit allows, up to 3,000 tokens, at $0.10 / $0.40 per million tokens. Caps from $0.002 to $0.06, drawn from a fixed seed. A client resends a 429 up to 50 times, 100 ms apart.

```text
cargo test --release --lib step_cap_proof -- --ignored --nocapture   (PYGG_PROOF_OUT=FILE for one row per step)
```

| Scenario | Steps × requests | What it tries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| oversized | 10 × 10, one after another | Output limits absent, 64,000 or 200,000 tokens, in either Chat Completions field; prompts up to 40,000 characters |
| concurrent | 20 × 25 at once | Two keys naming the same step |
| cancelled | 10 × 20 at once | Slow streams the client leaves after 20–720 ms |
| restart | 10 × 20 | Streams running when the gateway stops and starts again; a crashed gateway's unsettled request left in the journal |

## Results

10 runs after the fix (runs 21–30):

| Scenario | Requests | Answered | Refused (402) | Client left | Violations | Highest share of a cap used | Median share used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| oversized | 1,000 | 840 | 160 | 0 | 0 | 99.8% | 56.6% |
| concurrent | 5,000 | 3,696 | 1,304 | 0 | 0 | 99.95% | 97.4% |
| cancelled | 2,000 | 0 | 584 | 1,416 | 0 | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| restart | 2,000 | 1,144 | 24 | 832 | 0 | 99.2% | 71.4% |

- **Before the fix:** 2 of 12 runs had one step over its cap, by 1 and 2 micro-dollars: the provider billed 6–7 micro-dollars more than the ledger had counted over 8–9 requests (`before-fix-run-7.jsonl`, `before-fix-run-9.jsonl`). After it, the provider's bill never exceeded the ledger's.
- **Cancelled streams count at their hold.** Their usage never arrives, so the step is charged what it held, which the provider's bill never exceeded: a step can end with less spent than counted.
- **Not covered:** a provider that bills past the output limit it was sent (some reasoning models count thinking outside it), a provider's own price changing between the hold and the bill, and an agent's requests that do not go through Pygg.
